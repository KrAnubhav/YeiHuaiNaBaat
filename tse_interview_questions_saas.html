🛠️ Troubleshooting Scenarios

Most common — how you diagnose and solve issues live on a call with a client

Q1🔥 TOP
Client says: "I clicked the Download PDF button in Salesforce but nothing is happening. This is your product's bug."
📍 Scenario: Feature not working · L1 Classic
▾
Your Framework: Acknowledge → Clarify → Isolate → Fix/Escalate → Follow-up
✅ Model Answer Script
You: "Thank you for reaching out, [Name]. I completely understand how frustrating it must be when a feature you rely on stops working — let's get this sorted out right away."
You: "May I ask a few quick questions to help me pinpoint this faster? Which browser are you using, and when did this first happen? Has it ever worked before for you?"
Client: "I use Chrome. It worked last week. Now it just doesn't do anything when I click."
You: "Got it. Let's try a couple of quick steps — first, could you do a hard refresh with Ctrl+Shift+R and try again? Also, could you try it in an Incognito window to rule out any browser extensions?"
Client: "Tried both — still not working."
You: "Understood. Could you check if there's a pop-up blocker active? PDF downloads often get blocked silently. Also, I'll check on our end if there's a known issue with this feature — could you share your Org ID so I can look into your specific configuration?"
🔑 Key Skills Being Tested
Structured TroubleshootingEmpathy FirstBrowser/Environment CheckAsk Right Questions
⚠️ What NOT to Say
🚫"Are you sure you clicked it correctly?" — Never question their basic competence
🚫"That's not our bug." — Never dismiss before investigating
🚫"I don't know." — Say "Let me find out" instead
Q2🔥 TOP
Client says: "Your system has been down for 2 hours. I have a product demo in 30 minutes and I can't log into Salesforce. Fix it NOW."
📍 Scenario: Outage during critical moment · High urgency
▾
Your Framework: Validate urgency → Check status page → Offer workaround → Set timeline → Escalate
✅ Model Answer Script
You: "I hear you, [Name], and I understand the urgency completely — a demo in 30 minutes is critical. Let me treat this as a Priority 1 right now."
You: "I'm checking our status page as we speak — I can see there is currently a service disruption affecting some regions. Our engineering team is actively working on it. I want to be honest with you — I cannot guarantee it will be restored in 30 minutes."
You: "However, here's what I can do right now: First, let me check if the issue is region-specific — if you can try accessing through our mobile app or a different network, that may work. Second, I can escalate this immediately with your urgency flagged. Third, I'll stay on this call with you until we have a resolution or a clear workaround."
Client: "This is completely unacceptable."
You: "I completely understand your frustration, and I sincerely apologize for the impact this is causing. I'm escalating this right now and will update you every 10 minutes on the status. Is there anything else I can help prepare in the meantime — like offline materials for your demo?"
🔑 Key Skills Being Tested
Urgency RecognitionHonest CommunicationWorkaround ThinkingEscalation Speed
🚫Promise a fixed time if you don't know — "It'll be up in 10 mins" when you don't know
🚫Put them on hold without a reason or timeline
Q3🔥 TOP
Client says: "I set up a workflow automation in Salesforce yesterday but it's not triggering. I followed the documentation exactly."
📍 Scenario: Configuration issue · User may have missed a step
▾
Your Framework: Don't assume user error → Walk through config together → Find gap → Fix without blame
✅ Model Answer Script
You: "Thanks for reaching out. Automation issues can be tricky — let's walk through the setup together to make sure everything is configured correctly. Sometimes it's a small checkbox that's easy to miss."
You: "Could you share your screen or walk me through the workflow settings? I'd like to check: Is the workflow status set to Active? What is the trigger condition set to — record creation, edit, or both?"
Client: "I see it's set to Active. The trigger is on record edit."
You: "Perfect. One common thing to check — is 'Re-evaluate rules after field changes' turned on? If that's off, the rule won't re-trigger after an edit. Let's check that."
Client: "Oh — that was turned off. I turned it on and tested — it works now!"
You: "Excellent! That's a common one — the documentation doesn't always highlight it. I'll also send you a quick summary of what we changed so you have it for future reference."
🔑 Key Skills Being Tested
No-blame approachProduct KnowledgeGuided TroubleshootingDocumentation follow-up
Q4📌 Common
Client says: "I can't import my CSV data into Salesforce. The import fails every time with no useful error message."
📍 Scenario: Data import failure · Classic L1
▾
✅ Model Answer Script
You: "Let's get that import working for you. Could you share the CSV file or a sample of it? I'd like to check: column headers match the field API names exactly, there are no special characters or blank rows, and the file is UTF-8 encoded. Also — how many rows does the file have?"
Client: "It has 5,000 rows. Here's the file."
You: "I can see the 'Phone' column header uses a different spelling than the Salesforce field name — that's likely causing the silent failure. Let me also check if there are any required fields missing in your CSV..."

▾

You: "That's definitely not right — let's figure out where the mismatch is. A few things to check: First, what date range filter is set on the dashboard? Sometimes it defaults to the wrong period. Second, which user role is the dashboard running as — if it's running as a specific user, it may only show records they own. Third, are the closed deals in 'Closed Won' status specifically?"

I will not only fix the immediate issue — I will also investigate why it came back and make sure it doesn't happen a third time. I'll personally follow up with you in 48 hours to confirm it's stable."

😤 Handling Angry & Difficult Clients

Emotional intelligence is tested here — stay calm, validate, never argue

Q1🔥 TOP
Client shouts: "I've been transferred 3 times. Nobody is helping me. I want to speak to your manager RIGHT NOW!"
📍 Scenario: Transfer fatigue · Demand for manager
▾
Rule: Never immediately transfer. Try to own the issue first. Show you are capable and empathetic before escalating.
✅ Model Answer Script
You: "I completely hear you, [Name], and I am truly sorry about that experience. Being transferred multiple times without a resolution is absolutely unacceptable, and I take full responsibility for making sure that stops right now."
You: "I want to personally own this issue for you — I'm not going to transfer you again. Can you give me just a couple of minutes to understand your issue fully? My goal is to resolve this on this call itself."
Client: "I've already explained it 3 times. I just want your manager."
You: "I completely understand. I will connect you with my supervisor. While I do that — may I share what I know about your issue so they can help you immediately without you having to repeat yourself again?"
Own the issue firstNever argue about escalationWarm transfer with context
🚫"My manager will tell you the same thing." — Dismissive and confrontational
🚫Immediately saying "Sure, holding on" — shows you can't handle the situation
Q2🔥 TOP
Client says: "Your product is garbage. I'm going to cancel my subscription and move to a competitor. Nothing ever works!"
📍 Scenario: Churn threat · High-emotion moment
▾
✅ Model Answer Script
You: "I hear how frustrated you are, and I'm not going to dismiss that. What you're feeling is valid — if something isn't working consistently, that's a real problem for your business."
You: "Before you make that decision, I would genuinely like to understand what's been going wrong. Not to talk you out of anything — but because I believe we can fix this, and I want the chance to try. Can you tell me what specific issues have been happening?"
Client: "The reporting feature has been broken for weeks and nobody fixed it."
You: "That's not acceptable, and I agree with you. Let me pull up all your open tickets right now. I want to understand exactly what was reported and why it wasn't resolved. I'm also going to flag this to my team lead to give this priority treatment. Would you be willing to give me 24 hours to come back to you with a clear action plan?"
Don't be defensiveRetain with action not wordsEscalate to retention team
Q3🔥 TOP
Client uses abusive/rude language towards you during the call. How do you handle it without losing professionalism?
📍 Scenario: Abusive client · Boundary setting
▾
Key: You can set boundaries professionally. You are not required to accept verbal abuse — but do it calmly and without escalating.
✅ Model Answer Script
You: "[Name], I understand you're very upset, and I genuinely want to help you resolve this. However, I want to make sure we can have a productive conversation — I'll be able to help you much more effectively that way."
You: "I'm fully committed to solving your issue. If you'd like to continue, I'm here. But if the language continues, I may need to end this call and arrange a callback — which I'd prefer not to do. What would you like to do?"
Calm boundary settingStay solution-focusedGive them the choice
🚫Argue back or match their energy
🚫Hang up without warning
🚫Apologize for their behavior — "I'm sorry you feel that way" is fine but don't let abuse continue
Q4📌 Common
Client says: "I've been waiting for a resolution for 5 days. Your team just keeps saying 'we're working on it.' I need answers."
📍 Scenario: Ticket stalled · Expectation management
▾
✅ Model Answer Script
You: "You have every right to be frustrated — 5 days without a resolution or a clear update is not the experience we want you to have. I'm going to look at your ticket right now while we're on the call."
You: "I can see this is currently with our engineering team. Here's what I'm going to do: I'll get a specific status update from the engineering team by end of day today and send it to you in writing. I'll also set up a daily update so you're never left without information again. Does that work for you?"
Proactive updatesSet specific commitmentsAcknowledge the failure
Q5🧩 Tricky
Client says: "I spoke to your colleague yesterday who promised me this would be fixed by noon today. It's 3 PM and nothing is fixed. Where is my fix?"
📍 Scenario: Broken promise by colleague · Ownership moment
▾
✅ Model Answer Script
You: "I sincerely apologize that we didn't deliver on what was committed to you — that commitment should have been met or you should have received an update. That's on us."
You: "I'm not going to make any more time commitments unless I'm 100% certain I can keep them. Let me check the current status of your issue right now — I want to give you an accurate picture of where things stand and what the realistic timeline is, not just what sounds good."
Own the colleague's promiseNo new false promisesTransparent status update

💳 Billing, Refund & Policy Scenarios

The client made a mistake but wants a refund — or disputes a charge. Policy vs empathy balance.

Q1🔥 TOP
Client says: "Money was deducted from my account due to a mistake I made but I want a full refund. I didn't mean to upgrade." You know it's client's fault. How do you handle this?
📍 Scenario: Client error · Refund demand · Policy conflict
▾
Balance: Policy must be upheld — but empathy and a goodwill gesture can save the relationship. Never say "it's your fault."
✅ Model Answer Script
Client: "I accidentally upgraded my plan. Please refund the extra charge — I didn't want this."
You: "I completely understand — accidental upgrades can happen, especially with a complex pricing page. Let me look at your account."
You: "I can see the upgrade was processed 3 days ago through your admin portal. Our standard policy is that upgrades are non-refundable once the billing cycle has started, as the additional capacity was made available to you immediately."
Client: "But I never used any of those extra features! This isn't fair."
You: "I hear you, and I want to find a fair solution. While I'm unable to process a full refund per our policy, here's what I can do: I can downgrade your plan effective immediately so you won't be charged again next cycle, and I'll check if I'm able to apply a credit to your next invoice as a goodwill gesture — let me check what I'm authorized to offer."
You: "Also, I'll make a note of this on your account and flag it to my supervisor. We want to keep you as a valued customer, and I want to make sure you feel heard even when the answer has to be 'no' on the refund."
Never say "your fault"Explain policy clearlyOffer a goodwill alternativeEscalate for exceptions
🚫"You clicked the button — it's your mistake, not ours." — Factual but relationship-destroying
🚫Promise a refund you're not authorized to give
🚫Argue with the client about who is at fault
Q2🔥 TOP
Client says: "I was charged twice for the same month. I need an immediate refund." But you verify only one charge on your system.
📍 Scenario: Billing dispute · Investigation needed
▾
✅ Model Answer Script
You: "I completely understand your concern — a double charge is serious and I want to investigate this right away. I can see on our end that only one charge of [amount] was processed on [date]. However, I don't want to dismiss your concern at all."
You: "Could you share a screenshot of your bank statement showing the two charges? Sometimes there's a pending authorization that looks like a charge but drops off in 3-5 business days. Once I see the bank statement, I can compare it with our payment records and give you a clear answer within [X hours]."
Investigate before decidingAsk for evidenceExplain pending auth concept
Q3📌 Common
Client says: "I cancelled my subscription 2 weeks ago but I still got charged. Your cancellation system is broken."
📍 Scenario: Post-cancellation charge · Process failure
▾
✅ Model Answer Script
You: "I apologize — if you cancelled and were still charged, that needs to be corrected immediately. Let me check your account... I can see a cancellation request was submitted on [date]. I want to verify if it was fully processed or if there was a gap. If the charge happened after a confirmed cancellation, you are absolutely entitled to a full refund and I will initiate that right now."
If company error → refund immediatelyVerify cancellation recordDon't make client fight for what they're owed
Q4🧩 Tricky
Client keeps calling back demanding a refund you already denied. They escalate every time. How do you handle persistent pressure?
📍 Scenario: Repeated escalation · Firmness with empathy
▾
✅ Model Answer Script
You: "I understand you feel strongly about this, and I respect that. I want to be transparent with you — I've reviewed your case in detail and our position on the refund hasn't changed. I know that's not what you want to hear."
You: "What I can do is formally escalate your case to our billing review team so you get an official written response. This is the highest level of review available for billing disputes. They will reach out within [X business days]. Would that be helpful?"
Be firm but kindOffer formal review pathDon't keep reopening the same decision

🚨 Escalation Scenarios

Know when to own it and when to pass it up — with a warm handoff every time

Q1🔥 TOP
How do you decide when to escalate a ticket vs when to handle it yourself? Walk me through your decision process.
📍 Concept question · Escalation judgment
▾
Escalate when: Issue is beyond your access level · Requires engineering/product team · SLA breach risk · Client demands it · Security/data issue
✅ Model Answer
"I try to own as much as possible at L1 — asking for help unnecessarily wastes everyone's time and delays the client. I escalate when: the fix requires backend access I don't have, it's a confirmed product bug, the issue is a potential data breach, the client's SLA is at risk and I need faster resources, or the client specifically and repeatedly requests a higher level. When I do escalate, I always give the next person full context — what was tried, what the client said, and what I've already ruled out — so the client doesn't have to repeat themselves."
Ownership firstContext-rich handoffProtect SLAs
Q2🔥 TOP
You're handling a call and realize this is a critical bug affecting all clients — not just this one. What do you do immediately?
📍 Scenario: Widespread bug discovery · Incident response
▾
✅ Model Answer Script
You to client: "I want to let you know — I believe this may be affecting more users than just you. I'm treating this as a high-priority incident. I'm going to flag this to our engineering and incident response team right now. I'll stay on this call with you, and I'll make sure you're one of the first to know when we have a resolution."
Internal action: Simultaneously: open a P1 ticket · alert your team lead · check status page · document steps to reproduce · note client details for impact assessment.
P1 = Immediate escalationInform client transparentlyIncident response process
Q3📌 Common
You escalated a ticket to L2 but L2 is taking too long and the client is following up repeatedly. What do you do?
📍 Scenario: Internal escalation stalling
▾
✅ Model Answer
"I don't just pass a ticket and forget it — the client is still my responsibility until the case is closed. If L2 is taking long, I would: check the ticket status and ping the L2 engineer directly for an update, get a realistic ETA and communicate it to the client proactively before they follow up, and if the SLA is breached, escalate to my team lead. I would never leave the client without an update."
Ticket ownership after escalationProactive client updatesSLA breach = immediate flag
Q4🧩 Tricky
Your manager/L2 team tells you "We can't fix this right now — just tell the client." How do you communicate this without losing the client's trust?
📍 Scenario: Delivering bad news from internal team
▾
✅ Model Answer Script
You: "I want to be transparent with you — our engineering team has reviewed your issue. It is a known defect and it is on our product roadmap, but I want to be honest: it is not scheduled for an immediate fix."
You: "I know that's not what you wanted to hear. Here's what I can offer in the meantime: a workaround that partially addresses the issue, and I'm going to log your business impact so your voice reaches the product team when they prioritize the fix. I'll also update you when a timeline is confirmed."
Honest communicationOffer workaroundAdvocate for client internally

💬 Communication & Soft Skills

How you say things matters as much as what you say

Q1🔥 TOP
How do you explain a complex technical issue (like an API failure) to a non-technical client without confusing them?
📍 Concept · Technical communication
▾
✅ Model Answer
"I first figure out their technical level in the first 30 seconds of the call. If they're non-technical, I use analogies. For example, for an API failure I would say: 'Think of an API like a waiter in a restaurant. Your app (the customer) placed an order, the waiter (API) went to the kitchen (our server), but the kitchen had a problem and didn't complete the order. We're working on fixing the kitchen right now.' I focus on: what it means for them, what I'm doing to fix it, and when they can expect it resolved — not on the technical details."
Gauge audience firstUse real analogiesFocus on impact not tech
Q2🔥 TOP
Client says "I need this fixed ASAP." How do you manage their expectation when you know it will take 3-5 business days?
📍 Expectation management · Timeline communication
▾
✅ Model Answer Script
You: "I completely understand the urgency. I want to give you an honest and accurate timeline rather than a promise I can't keep. Based on the complexity of this issue, our engineering team estimates 3-5 business days for a complete resolution."
You: "What I can do right now: Is there a workaround that can unblock you in the meantime? And I'll set up daily updates so you always know where things stand. I'd rather give you a realistic timeline and beat it than give you a short one and miss it."
Honest over optimisticOffer interim workaroundDaily updates commitment

⚙️ Technical Knowledge Questions

Core technical concepts a TSE at SaaS companies must know

Q1🔥 TOP
What is an API? How would you explain it to a client whose integration has broken?
📍 API basics · Client explanation
▾
✅ Technical + Client Explanation
Technical: An API (Application Programming Interface) is a set of rules that allows two software systems to communicate. It defines requests, responses, data formats, and authentication methods.
To client: "Think of our API like a power socket in a wall. Your application is the appliance — as long as the plug fits the socket (correct format), everything works. When an integration breaks, it usually means something changed on either end — like the plug or socket changed shape. Let me check if there was a recent API update that might have caused this."
REST vs SOAP basicsAPI key / auth tokensStatus codes (200, 401, 404, 500)
Q2🔥 TOP
Client reports "Error 403 Forbidden" when accessing a Salesforce record. What are the possible causes and how do you troubleshoot?
📍 HTTP errors · Permission troubleshooting
▾
✅ Model Answer
403 Forbidden = You are authenticated but not authorized.
Possible causes: User's profile doesn't have access to that object/field · Record sharing rules are restricting access · Organization-wide defaults (OWD) set to Private · Connected app doesn't have required permissions scope · IP restriction is blocking the request.
Troubleshooting steps: Check user's Profile and Permission Sets · Review sharing rules and OWD settings · Check Connected App OAuth scopes · Review login/access history in Setup.
403 = Auth not AccessProfile & Permission SetsSharing Rules & OWD
Q3📌 Common
What is the difference between a bug, a feature request, and a configuration issue? Why does it matter in support?
📍 Ticket classification · Core TSE skill
▾
✅ Model Answer
Bug: Product is not behaving as designed/documented. Must be escalated to engineering. Client is not at fault.
Configuration Issue: Product is working correctly but the client's setup is wrong. Resolved at L1/L2 support level. Requires guidance and education.
Feature Request: The product doesn't support what the client wants to do. Logged for product team — not a bug, not fixable immediately.
Why it matters: Wrong classification wastes everyone's time. A bug sent as feature request = client waits forever. A config issue escalated to engineering = engineering time wasted.
Bug → EngineeringConfig → Support GuideFeature → Product Team
Q4📌 Common
What steps do you take when you receive a ticket you have absolutely no knowledge about?
📍 Unknown issue handling · Learning approach
▾
✅ Model Answer
"First, I acknowledge receipt and set an honest response time expectation with the client. Then: 1) Check internal knowledge base and documentation, 2) Search previous similar tickets to see if this was solved before, 3) Reproduce the issue in a sandbox if possible, 4) Ask a senior colleague or team lead, 5) If still stuck, escalate with all context I've gathered. I never tell a client I don't know — I say 'I'm looking into this and will get back to you by [time]'."
KB firstPast ticketsReproduce in sandboxAsk then escalate
Q5📌 Common
What is an SLA? How do you ensure you meet SLAs when handling multiple tickets simultaneously?
📍 SLA management · Prioritization
▾
✅ Model Answer
SLA (Service Level Agreement): A committed response/resolution time based on ticket priority. E.g., P1 = 1 hour response, P2 = 4 hours, P3 = 1 business day.
How I manage: Triage first — identify P1s immediately. Use ticketing tool filters to sort by SLA breach risk. Set personal reminders before SLA expires. Update clients proactively before they follow up. If I'm at risk of breaching, communicate early and ask for help — never let an SLA breach silently.
P1 always firstProactive client updatesAsk for help before breach

🧠 Behavioral Interview Questions

STAR method — Situation, Task, Action, Result

Q1🔥 TOP
Tell me about a time you dealt with a very angry or frustrated customer. What did you do?
📍 STAR · Empathy · De-escalation
▾
STAR Formula: S = Situation · T = Task · A = Action · R = Result
✅ Sample Answer Structure
S: "During my internship, a client called very upset because their data export had failed for 3 consecutive days and they had a board presentation the next morning."
T: "My task was to resolve the data export issue within hours, and also calm the client who was threatening to escalate."
A: "I first acknowledged their frustration genuinely — not scripted. I then immediately reproduced the issue, found that a scheduled maintenance had changed an API endpoint, and worked with the backend team to run a manual export. I stayed on the call with the client throughout and gave them a 15-minute update."
R: "The client received their data 2 hours later. They were grateful and ended up submitting a positive survey. I also documented the root cause so future clients wouldn't face the same issue."
Empathy firstOwnership through resolutionPrevent recurrence
Q2🔥 TOP
Tell me about a time you didn't know the answer to a client's question. How did you handle it?
📍 Honesty · Learning on the job
▾
✅ Key Points to Cover
• You were transparent — said "I'll find out" not "I don't know"
• You set a clear response timeline with the client
• You used knowledge base, documentation, or a colleague
• You came back to the client on time with the answer
• You documented the answer for future use
Never fake an answerCommit to a callback timeBuild the KB for others
Q3📌 Common
How do you prioritize when you have 10 open tickets and 3 clients calling at the same time?
📍 Multitasking · Prioritization
▾
✅ Model Answer
"I triage by urgency and SLA breach risk first — P1 tickets (service down, revenue impacted) always get priority. For the 3 simultaneous calls: I'd take the most urgent one, briefly acknowledge the others are waiting (if there's a queue system), handle one at a time, and ask a colleague to cover if the volume exceeds what I can manage alone. I use the ticketing tool to set priority flags so nothing falls through."
SLA triage firstOne call at a timeAsk for team help
Q4📌 Common
Why do you want to work in Technical Support? Isn't it stressful dealing with angry clients all day?
📍 Motivation · Role fit
▾
✅ Model Answer
"I genuinely enjoy problem-solving and human interaction — technical support combines both. The stress comes from not having answers — I manage that by building my knowledge base continuously and knowing when to ask for help. When I turn a frustrated client into a satisfied one, that's incredibly rewarding. I also see technical support as the best way to deeply understand a product — it's a foundation for roles in CS, product, or engineering over time."
Passion not just jobLong-term career thinkingStress management
Q5📌 Common
Tell me about a time you went above and beyond for a client.
📍 Ownership · Initiative
▾
✅ What to Highlight

👔 Hiring Manager (HM) Round Questions

Deeper, strategic, culture-fit questions — HM is checking for attitude, growth mindset, and client-first thinking

Q1👔 HM
What does "excellent customer support" mean to you? How is it different from just "good" support?
📍 Philosophy · Values alignment
▾
✅ Model Answer
"Good support resolves the problem. Excellent support resolves the problem, prevents it from recurring, communicates proactively, and leaves the client feeling genuinely valued — not just processed. The difference is the human element. Excellent support means the client hangs up thinking 'that person actually cared about my issue' — not just 'they fixed it.' I also think excellent support means contributing to the knowledge base so your teammates can solve the same issue faster next time."

Q2👔 HM
A client is 100% wrong but 100% convinced they are right. Our policy doesn't allow a refund. How do you handle this without damaging the relationship?
📍 Policy vs empathy · HM classic
▾
✅ Model Answer
"I would never argue about who is 'right.' I'd start by fully acknowledging their frustration and genuinely trying to see it from their perspective — sometimes their logic makes sense even if the facts don't support their conclusion. Then I explain the policy clearly, in plain language, without legalese. I always offer something — even if it's not a refund. A credit, a downgrade, a feature, a free session — something that shows we value them. I escalate if needed. And I document the case so the product or policy team gets this feedback."
Never argue right/wrongAcknowledge fully firstAlways offer an alternativeFeedback loops to product

Q4👔 HM
How do you handle stress and avoid burnout when dealing with emotionally demanding clients all day?
📍 Resilience · Self-management
▾
✅ Model Answer
"I separate the situation from the person — when a client is angry, they are angry at the situation, not at me. That mental shift helps a lot. I also practice what I call 'emotional reset' between calls — even 30 seconds of a quick break helps. I build resilience by focusing on outcomes — every resolved ticket is a win. And I lean on my team — venting briefly to a colleague after a tough call is healthy. Long term, I stay motivated by tracking wins, not just difficult cases."
Don't take it personallyEmotional reset between callsTeam support

Q6👔 HM
You have 5 open P2 tickets. Your manager asks you to stop and join a 2-hour training session. What do you do?
📍 Conflict of priorities · Manager relationship
▾
✅ Model Answer
"I'd quickly check the SLA breach risk on my 5 open tickets. If none are near their SLA deadline, I'd ask a colleague to monitor them, send quick acknowledgement updates to each client, and join the training. If one is close to breaching, I'd briefly inform my manager — 'I have a P2 closing on SLA in 45 minutes, can I step out briefly or can someone cover?' I wouldn't skip training without communication, and I wouldn't miss an SLA breach without flagging it."
Check SLA firstCommunicate with managerAsk for coverage not permission to fail
Q7👔 HM
If you could redesign one thing about how support teams work to make them better for clients, what would it be?
📍 Thinking beyond your role · Innovation mindset
▾
✅ Model Answer
"I would eliminate ticket transfers as much as possible — every transfer is a trust erosion moment. I'd design support so the first person who picks up the ticket owns it end-to-end, with access to escalate internally while remaining the client's single point of contact. The client should always have one name they can go back to. This also forces engineers to become more T-shaped — broad enough to handle most issues, deep on their specialty."
Single point of contactSystemic thinkingClient experience first
